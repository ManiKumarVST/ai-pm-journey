## Section 1 — Quote Extraction

### Interview A — Rohan Verma (SDE, Bangalore, WFH)

- **Quote:** "easily 5-6 times. WFH plus late meetings — cooking is impossible"
  - **Signals:** A frequent user of Zomato and busy due to profession. Rohan relies on food deliveries and expects a seamless service due to busy schedule.

- **Quote:** "Zomato has become... unreliable. Especially evenings. Like yesterday — I ordered biryani at 8:30 PM. App promised 30 minutes. It arrived at 9:45 PM. Cold."
  - **Signals:** In the evenings, there is huge gap between Promise time and Delivery time. The gap is ~1.5X of the Promise time due to which Zomato is considered as unreliable option in the evening times.

- **Quote:** "75 minutes! And this isn't a one-off. I've timed it. Between 7-9 PM in Bangalore, if I order Zomato, I'm gambling. 60-70 minute deliveries are the norm, not the exception."
  - **Signals:** Delivery times in Bangalore are trending ~60-70 minutes between 7-9 PM.

- **Quote:** "First 1-2 times, I raised tickets. Got 50 rupees credit. Whatever. Now I just accept it. Or cancel and switch to Swiggy."
  - **Signals:** Not satisfactory resolution from Customer service. Getting only 50 rupees in credit.

- **Quote:** "When Gold was 200 rupees for unlimited free delivery, no restrictions, it was a no-brainer. Now they've added minimum order values, restricted restaurants, handling fees Gold doesn't cover... my monthly savings dropped from about 500 rupees to 150. Membership was 200. Math didn't work anymore."
  - **Signals:** User not finding value in Gold subscription as Zomato started adding additional charges which are making the savings drop.

### Interview B — Meera Krishnan (Restaurant owner, Chennai)

- **Quote:** "We've been marked 'low fulfillment' twice. Our visibility drops. Ratings suffer. But what can we do? Sunday afternoon at 1 PM, I have 60 dine-in customers waiting. A delivery order comes in — someone in HSR wants 4 uttapams. If I take it, one of my walk-in customers waits 90 minutes. If I cancel, one delivery guy gets annoyed. It's arithmetic."
  - **Signals:** The user is getting marked "low fulfilment" in the weekends around lunch time. User wants to prioritize dine-in customers at that hours and want to limit deliveries.

- **Quote:** "Their account manager comes once every 2-3 months. I've told him five times. Nothing changes."
  - **Signals:** No proper resolution when escalated to Account manager.

- **Quote:** "The app just gives me one option — mark yourself offline. But then my ratings drop because I've been 'unavailable' too much."
  - **Signals:** Existing feature of marking offline is indirectly creating trouble for the user — making the option unreliable.

- **Quote:** "100 percent. I'd rather take 5 delivery orders per hour and deliver ALL 5 in 30 minutes, than accept 15 and cancel 10."
  - **Signals:** User wants to optimize the supply during peak hours that will reduce fulfilment rate.

- **Quote:** "Peak hour + delivery = cancellation. It's the same story everywhere. Zomato's team doesn't get it because they've never run a kitchen."
  - **Signals:** User is considering this is a widespread issue across all restaurants. However, Zomato isn't considering this as a critical problem.

### Interview C — Priya Nair (Marketing Exec, Mumbai)

- **Quote:** "That leaves me 5k for everything else — including any food out or delivery."
  - **Signals:** User is calculative in nature. User allocates monthly budget for every spend and will manage everything within the budget. If the budget exhausts, the user will stop ordering online.

- **Quote:** "Suddenly it's not 200. It's basically 260. That's almost 30 percent more than I thought I was paying."
  - **Signals:** The final bill amount is much higher than what user thought it would be based on item price.

- **Quote:** "40 rupees is a vada pav. Two of them, even. Why am I paying vada-pav-price for someone to bring my food?"
  - **Signals:** As user is calculative, User compares every rupee spent against the deliveries. High delivery charges — User will not order online at all.

- **Quote:** "This meal, all-in, is 245 rupees. I decide once."
  - **Signals:** User is expecting transparency much earlier in the funnel that will enable faster decisioning to order.

- **Quote:** "My friend Karthik told me the value has dropped a lot in the past few months."
  - **Signals:** User considering the Gold subscription is not much valuable as a friend suggested not to go for it — indicates poor word of mouth for Gold subscription.

### Interview D — Karthik Reddy (PM, Bangalore, ex-Gold)

- **Quote:** "Suddenly it was 'Gold-eligible restaurants only' — they cut the eligible list by like 40 percent. Then minimum order value went up from 199 to 249. Then they added a 'handling fee' that Gold didn't cover. Then peak-hour surcharges Gold didn't cover either."
  - **Signals:** User not finding Gold subscription valuable because the eligibility changed to selected restaurants only and the minimum order value/additional charges are reducing the savings.

- **Quote:** "On the cancellation screen — you know Zomato — they showed me 'You've saved ₹2,100 with Gold this year!' Which is true! But the RATE of savings had collapsed. That's what mattered to me. Not what I saved, but what I would save going forward."
  - **Signals:** User is clear that Gold subscription gives some level of savings but the concern is that the effective savings got reduced.

- **Quote:** "I still order — maybe 15 times a month. But I feel different about them."
  - **Signals:** User is still loyal to Zomato but is clear that the value user is getting through Zomato is reduced.

- **Quote:** "'Gold is going up to 400 rupees, here's why, here's the new value structure' — I might've paid it if the new value was clear. But hiding changes behind fine print? That's a trust break."
  - **Signals:** As a loyal customer of Zomato, if the user gets the transparency behind high prices, the user wouldn't have churned from Gold subscription.

- **Quote:** "Swiggy One is 599 for 3 months but the coverage is broader. Testing it. If it holds, I'll shift more of my orders. My data lock-in with Zomato is real — 6 years of order history — but not infinite."
  - **Signals:** Competitor giving clear value with broader coverage. Zomato's order history is a big moat for user to stick with it.

## Section 4 — Problem Statements

### Problem Statement 1

> When ordering dinner between 7-9 PM in Bangalore, urban working professionals (25-35) want reliable delivery within promised time, but Zomato consistently delivers in 45-75 min against a 30-min promise — evidenced by 131 QuickBites orders averaging 61 min AND 3/6 interviews (Rohan, Anjali, Vikram) explicitly citing evening-hour delivery as their #1 pain.

### Problem Statement 2

> When a customer is ordering for the first time, the user wants the food to be delivered on time but the orders are getting delayed — evidenced by 23/46 orders delayed by more than 15 minutes AND 2/6 interviews (Anjali, Vikram) explicitly cited terrible first order experience.

### Problem Statement 3

> When ordering food from certain restaurants, users want the food to be delivered warm and fresh, but for certain restaurants the complaints are high — evidenced by 2/8 restaurants having 59% of complaints AND 3/6 users (Rohan, Anjali, Vikram) explicitly mentioned in interview that they received food cold.
